The dissertation sources identify and categorize the parts of Sinhala letterforms into several layers, ranging from linguistic structural components to fine anatomical details.

1\. Linguistic and Structural ComponentsThese parts represent the primary building blocks of the Sinhala writing system:

*   **Independent Vowels**: Characters that represent a vowel sound when placed at the beginning of a word.
*   **Consonants**: The core symbols of the script, each possessing an inherent "a" vowel sound.
*   **Vowel Signs (Pili or Pillama)**: Dependent marks attached to consonants to alter their inherent vowel sound. These include specific marks like the **Aspilla** (upper vowel mark) and **Paapilla** (lower vowel mark).
*   **Virama (Al-Lakuna)**: A special sign used to mark the absence of the inherent vowel. It takes two forms: a **flag** appearance (ක්) or a **cap** appearance (ම්) for characters with counter-clockwise curved strokes.
*   **Non-Alphabetical Consonant Signs**: Compositions used in practice but not included in the standard alphabet, primarily the **Yanshaya**, **Repaya**, and **Rakaranshaya**.
*   **Combined/Conjunct Consonants (Baddha Akshara)**: Devices where two or more consonants are combined into one unit to reduce space.2\. Anatomical Features (Stroke Primitives)The dissertations identify specific "stroke primitives" that define the visual anatomy of the characters:

*   **Eye (Akshi)**: A curved stroke that completes itself into a small counter space. Experts categorize these as **Opened (Vivṛta)**, **Closed (Sanvṛta)**, **Horned (Śṛṅgika)**, **Trunk (Śuṇḍa)**, or **Navel (Nābhi)**.
*   **Base Stroke or Body (Skandha)**: The main structural part of the character sitting on the baseline. It can be **Full (Pùrṇa)**, **Frontward-backward (Pùrva-apara)**, **Broken (Bāṅgika)**, or **Pointed (Śulita)**.
*   **Ascender Stroke (Arohana)**: Strokes that extend from the middle of the letter toward the top ascending line.
*   **Descender Stroke (Avarohana)**: Strokes that extend from the baseline toward the bottom descending line.
*   **Knot (Grantika)**: A feature formed where strokes overlap or cross over each other.
*   **Hook or Bearer (Dhāraka)**: An intermediating stroke that joins different parts, such as connecting an eyeball to an ascender stroke.
*   **Hump (Gaṇḍa)**: A horizontal stroke parallel to the baseline that joins two halves of a character.
*   **Stem or Arm (Bāhuva)**: Straight strokes that can be **Horizontal (Tiryak)**, **Fallen/Vertical (Avanata)**, or **Upward (Unnata)**.
*   **Spiral (Śṅkaya)**: A clockwise rotating stroke constructed at the base height.
*   **Shoulder (Uro)**: A curved stroke composed parallel to the base stroke, either as a joint or an adjacent stroke.
*   **Loop (Pāsha)**: Formed when a stroke changes direction and overlaps without crossing; these are located in the **Ascending**, **Middle**, or **Descending** zones.3\. Joinery and TerminalsThese parts define how the anatomical strokes are connected or concluded:

*   **Eye Joint (Akshi Dhārak Sandi)**: The point where the "eye" connects to the preceding strokes.
*   **Curve to Curve Joint (Dvivakra Sandi)**: The collision point of two circular shapes.
*   **Right Angular Joint (Chandra Vaṇka Sandi)**: A connection between two characters using right-angled strokes.
*   **Intersections (Vedaka)**: The point where two separate strokes overlap.
*   **Terminals (Agra)**: The specific ways in which the character strokes terminate or end.
